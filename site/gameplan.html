Matchup model · Series prep

Series Game Plan: Opening Day 2027

Pick a Royals starter and one of the first four 2027 opponents. A pitch-level model scores every pitch in the starter's real 2026 arsenal, in 13 locations and all 12 counts, against each hitter's profile: bat speed, swing length, attack angle, chase, whiff by pitch type, and contact quality. Small samples are shrunk toward league average. The plan shows what to throw, where, and in which count. Values are runs per 100 pitches compared with the pitcher's usual mix in that count against that hitter, from the pitcher's side (+ means good for the pitcher).

Three things for the staff

Hitter-by-hitter plan

Lineup = Click a hitter for his zone map and profile. Recommendations only use locations where this pitcher actually throws that pitch at least of the time, so every plan is one he can execute. "Avoid" lists his own frequent habits (spots that get + of that pitch) that grade worst against this hitter.

What the model learned about hitter types

This is a partial-dependence check. Take the same set of real 2026 pitches and hold every hitter trait at league average except one. Then set that trait to a typical bottom-quartile value and a typical top-quartile value for regular hitters, and re-score. The empirical columns are the actual run values on those pitches against bottom- and top-quartile hitters. They're noisier and confounded, but they show the model isn't inventing effects. Runs per 100 pitches, pitcher's view.

Validation: honest numbers

Time split: trained on games before , tested on everything after. Hitter profiles for the test came only from the training window, the same information a staff would have had at the time. The target is the project's de-noised run value: balls in play are valued by exit velocity, launch angle, and count rather than by whether they fell in.

Calibration on the test window

Test pitches split into deciles by predicted value (count effect removed). If the model is calibrated, the dots sit on the diagonal.
