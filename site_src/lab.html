Pitch Design Lab

Pick a Royals pitch, then move the sliders to see how the Stuff+ model grades a changed shape. The model runs in your browser: it is this project's xgboost Stuff+ model (shape only, no location, no results), exported tree by tree from R. Each slider shifts all of his real pitches of that type (up to 150 sampled pitches) by the same amount, and the grade is the average prediction. These are model estimates, not promises of real-world results.

His own pitch is left out. Stuff+ is the published grade. How to read this, and its limits

  • Stuff+: 100 = average MLB pitch, 10 points = 1 SD across pitcher-pitch types with 100+ pitches. The model targets expected run value per pitch from shape alone, and that signal is weak per pitch (held-out pitch-level R² ≈ 0.1%). It is useful for ranking pitch types, but don't read small changes as precise.
  • The interval is a bootstrap over his sampled pitches. It shows how much the grade depends on which pitches were sampled, not the model's own error, so the true uncertainty is larger.
  • Sliders move one thing at a time, so they can make shapes that don't exist in real life. In reality spin, axis, arm angle and movement move together, and spin axis and arm angle stay fixed here. Release height and side change only the release features, not his arm angle.
  • Off-speed differentials: for a secondary pitch, changing velo or movement also changes its gap to his primary fastball, which the model uses. Changing the primary fastball itself leaves those gaps unchanged in this tool, but in real life every other pitch's gap would move too.
