1 · A simple, honest Stuff+

Question: how good is a pitch on shape alone, before location and luck?

Method (R, xgboost): one gradient-boosted model per pitch group (fastballs, breaking, offspeed), trained on all MLB pitches. Inputs are physical only: velocity, IVB, HB, spin rate, spin axis, extension, release height and side, arm angle, and each pitch's velocity and movement gap from the pitcher's primary fastball. Lefties are mirrored so arm side is always +. The target is expected run value: for balls in play, the actual result is swapped for the league-average value of that exit velocity, launch angle and count. That strips out defense and BABIP noise. Grades are scaled so 100 = MLB average pitch and 10 = one standard deviation across pitcher-pitch types.

Validation: models were trained only on games through and tested on everything after. A single pitch's run value is mostly noise, so pitch-level R² is tiny (that's normal and expected). The real test is predictive: did early-season Stuff+ predict late-season run value better than early-season results did? It did (chart, right).

Limits, stated plainly: this is a deliberately simple public-data model. There's no pitch-tracking-only data (seam-shifted wake, true spin efficiency), no catcher or park adjustment, and one season of data. Treat a grade as a starting point for a conversation, not a verdict. Being honest about uncertainty is part of the job.

2 · Vertical approach angle: where flat fastballs play

VAA is the angle the ball is travelling as it crosses the front of the plate, computed from Statcast's trajectory fit (vy0, vz0, ay, az). Raw VAA mostly reflects where a pitch is thrown (r² = against plate height), so I regress it on location and keep the residual: location-adjusted VAA. Positive means flatter than expected for that height.

Across all 2026 four-seamers, the flattest fifth got whiffs per swing at the top of the zone, vs for the steepest fifth. At the bottom of the zone the gap nearly disappears ( vs ). Flat fastballs are a top-of-the-zone weapon.

4 · Tunneling: what it buys and what it doesn't

For every pair of consecutive pitches in an at-bat, I rebuilt both trajectories from the Statcast 9-parameter fit. Then I measured how far apart the two pitches were at the tunnel point (23.8 ft in front of the plate, about when a hitter has to commit) and again at the plate.

For fastball-then-breaking-ball pairs across MLB, tight tunnels go with far more chases: 51% when the two pitches are within 2 inches at the tunnel point, vs 24% at 16–18 inches. Whiff rate per swing rises slightly the other way. My read is that tunneling earns the swing decision, and movement after the tunnel point earns the miss. Location is a confounder (a pitch that tunnels with a strike usually starts near the zone), so I treat this as descriptive, not causal.

5 · Count leverage, with shrinkage

Usage and results by count group (ahead, even, behind, two strikes) for any pitcher. Small samples are pulled toward the league average for that pitch type and count group using a 250-pitch prior (empirical-Bayes style). A pitch type with 40 pitches barely moves from league average, while one with 600 mostly keeps its own number. This is what keeps a 25-pitch fluke from becoming a recommendation.

Left: pitch mix by count group. Right: expected run value per 100 pitches (pitcher's view, + = good). Hollow = raw, filled = shrunk, so you can see how much each estimate moves.

7 · Coach-facing recommendations

Each recommendation is written the way I'd bring it to the pitching coach: one change, why, and how confident I am. All numbers come from the 2026 data. Shrunk values use a 250-pitch prior toward the league average for that pitch type and batter side.

"His fastball is flat and 98. Live at the top of the zone, especially with two strikes."

What I'd do next with club data: confirm these with pitcher-level biomechanics and pitch-design notes, check catcher framing and the opposing lineups behind the samples, and talk to the pitcher about what he's trying to do. A recommendation that fights his plan or his feel won't stick.
